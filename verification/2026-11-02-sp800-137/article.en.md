# Information Security Continuous Monitoring (ISCM) for Federal Information Systems and Organizations (SP 800-137)

## principle

**Standard**: Information Security Continuous Monitoring (ISCM) for Federal Information Systems and Organizations (SP 800-137) (联邦信息与组织的信息安全持续监视（SP 800-137）). The subject definition sits in the Executive Summary at the front: "Information security continuous monitoring (ISCM) is defined as maintaining ongoing awareness of information security, vulnerabilities, and threats to support organizational risk management decisions." Section 1.3 gives the action of the document: "The purpose of this guideline is to assist organizations in the development of an ISCM strategy and the implementation of an ISCM program that provides awareness of threats and vulnerabilities, visibility into organizational assets, and the effectiveness of deployed security controls." The same paragraph states what it supports: "The ISCM strategy and program support ongoing assurance that planned and implemented security controls are aligned with organizational risk tolerance, as well as the ability to provide the information needed to respond to risk in a timely manner." The title block confines the object to the federal side: "Information Security Continuous Monitoring (ISCM) for Federal Information Systems and Organizations". The output is two-level, strategy and program: what is monitored, how often, and who judges it belong to those two levels, while implementing the controls is not.

## why

The argument gives the statutory floor first and then the operational reason. Section 1.1 records what the 2002 statute required: "The Federal Information Security Management Act (FISMA) of 2002 further emphasized the importance of continuously monitoring information system security by requiring agencies to conduct assessments of security controls at a frequency appropriate to risk, but no less than annually." It then records the reporting posture of the year: "The memorandum provides instructions for annual FISMA reporting and emphasizes monitoring the security state of information systems on an ongoing basis with a frequency sufficient to make ongoing, risk-based decisions." The Executive Summary supplies the resource constraint, "Timely, relevant, and accurate information is vital, particularly when resources are limited and agencies must prioritize their efforts." and the premise "the ability to manage this technology and to assure confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information is now also mission-critical." Read together, annual assessment is a floor rather than a sufficient frequency, and the changes and threats that arrive between point-in-time assessments need continuous visibility matched to risk.

## used

Who acts is carried by the role entries of §2.4 and the decision sentences at the system tier, stated as plain present tense rather than shall. Section 2.4 says of the chief information officer "The CIO ensures that an effective ISCM program is established and implemented for the organization" and of the authorizing official "reviews security status reports and critical security documents and determines if the risk to the organization from operation of the information system remains acceptable." Section 3.1 puts frequency decisions on the system owner: "System owners determine assessment frequencies of security controls based on drivers from all three tiers." and sets evidence delivery at a policy-determined floor: "Assessment evidence obtained from ISCM is, at a minimum, provided to AOs as often as required by organizational policy." The same section assigns the risk bar to the risk executive (function): "The risk executive (function) determines the overall organizational risk tolerance and risk mitigation strategy at the organization tier." The criteria sentence from §3.2.2 reports as §3.1.4 in this text: "Organizations take the following criteria into consideration when establishing monitoring frequencies for metrics or assessment frequencies for security controls:" one item of which is control volatility: "Volatile security controls are assessed more frequently, whether the objective is establishing security control effectiveness or supporting calculation of a metric." The obligation level is therefore this document describing duties plus organizational policy: the annual floor comes from FISMA, not from here.

## broken

The four limits work at different levels. Section 3.1 states its own non-prescriptiveness: "The guidelines below, though not prescriptive, helps to ensure an organization-wide approach to ISCM that best promotes standardized methodologies and consistent practices". The boundary of automation is conceded in the opening body of Chapter One, where the sentence prints after the CHAPTER ONE and INTRODUCTION headings and before 1.1 BACKGROUND on a page this copy still labels PAGE 1: "While this document encourages the use of automation, it is recognized that many aspects of ISCM programs are not easily automated." The Executive Summary separately prints must-level scope: "It is important to recognize that with any comprehensive information security program, all implemented security controls, including management and operational controls, must be regularly assessed for effectiveness, even if the monitoring of such controls cannot be automated or is not easily automated." Section 2.2 denies that frequencies are transferable constants: "Monitoring frequencies (e.g., annually, quarterly, monthly, daily) are not static, and they are not uniform across all metrics." The Authority block adds two bounds: "such standards and guidelines shall not apply to national security systems without the express approval of appropriate federal officials exercising policy authority over such systems." and "This publication may be used by nongovernmental organizations on a voluntary basis and is not subject to copyright in the United States." So the limits differ by level rather than by existence: Section 3.1 calls itself non-prescriptive while the Executive Summary still states a must-level assessment scope, so the guideline is not silent about obligations; automation does not reach every process, frequencies are set per organisation, national security systems need express approval, and non-governmental use is purely voluntary.

## history

The title block carries "NIST Special Publication 800-137", a part title that spells out ISCM and confines it to federal systems, and the author line naming eight people in order, "Kelley Dempsey Nirali Shah Chawla Arnold Johnson Ronald Johnston Alicia Clay Jones Angela Orebaugh Matthew Scholl Kevin Stine", with the date line set in capitals on the title block and the signing officials below it, of whom "Patrick D. Gallagher, Under Secretary for Standards and Technology and Director". The Authority page gives the basis, "This publication has been developed by NIST to further its statutory responsibilities under the Federal Information Security Management Act (FISMA), Public Law (P.L.) 107-347.", then puts the extent and the date on two separate printed lines: "NIST Special Publication 800-137, 80 pages" and, on the next line, the date in parentheses (September 2011). The page count and the date are not run together in this copy. The acknowledgements split the eight authors by employer: "The authors, Kelley Dempsey, Arnold Johnson, Matthew Scholl and Kevin Stine of the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), Ronald Johnston of the Department of Defense Chief Information Officer, Defense-wide Information Assurance Program (DOD-CIO, DIAP), Alicia Clay Jones and Angela Orebaugh of Booz Allen Hamilton, and Nirali Shah Chawla of PricewaterhouseCoopers LLP (PwC), wish to thank their colleagues who reviewed drafts of this document and contributed to its technical content." This copy has no DOI line and no revision or supersession statement; the extraction also drops interior hyphens from headings (IS CM, RES PONS IBILITIES), so no edition fact is taken from a heading.
