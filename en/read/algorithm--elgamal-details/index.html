ElGamal Public-Key Encryption

Article review and catalog-metadata acceptance are recorded separately. The library remains under construction.

Principles

Algorithm: ElGamal (ElGamal 公钥密码). ### From Diffie–Hellman to encryption

ElGamal encryption turns a Diffie–Hellman shared secret into a one-time mask. The public parameters are a cyclic group G of order q, a generator g, and a public key h=g^x; the private key x stays secret. Encryption picks a random k and computes c1=g^k, c2=m·h^k; decryption computes m=c2·(c1^x)^{-1}. ### Why it is correct

c1^x=(g^k)^x=g^{kx}=h^k, so the mask h^k cancels. A message must first be encoded as a group element; arbitrary bytes cannot simply be multiplied as if they were group elements. ### How it is used

Bare ElGamal is rarely used to encrypt long messages. Real systems use it as a KEM or inside hybrid encryption: ElGamal wraps a symmetric key, and an AEAD such as AES-GCM protects the body. The elliptic-curve variant is commonly called ECIES or DHIES.

Why it matters

It turns “computing discrete logarithms is hard” into usable public-key encryption. Unlike RSA, which relies on integer factorization, ElGamal relies on the discrete-logarithm problem in a finite field or elliptic curve. The random value k makes every encryption of the same plaintext different.

The price is a ciphertext roughly twice the size of the plaintext, two modular exponentiations for encryption and one for decryption. It is far slower than symmetric cryptography, so its practical role is key encapsulation and hybrid encryption, not bulk data.

Applications

GnuPG and PGP ElGamal keys and the DSA signature scheme are tied to the same family of discrete-logarithm problems; elliptic-curve ElGamal variants appear in ECIES and similar hybrid schemes. TLS 1.3 does not use bare ElGamal: it uses ECDHE for key agreement and AEAD for record protection.

Do not confuse ElGamal encryption with ElGamal signatures. They share a hardness assumption but solve different protocol problems.

Limits & attacks

Textbook ElGamal is malleable: multiplying c2 by t produces a valid ciphertext for t·m. It is therefore not chosen-ciphertext secure and must not be presented as authenticated encryption.

Implementations must validate that group elements lie in the correct subgroup, avoid small-subgroup attacks, use audited group parameters or curves, and wrap the primitive in an authenticated KEM/DEM, DHIES or ECIES construction rather than exposing the raw transform to untrusted input.

History

Taher ElGamal presented the scheme at CRYPTO 1984 and published it in IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 31(4):469–472 in 1985. It adapted Diffie–Hellman key agreement into public-key encryption and also introduced a signature scheme that became an important influence on DSA.

ElGamal encryption and ElGamal signatures share the discrete-logarithm setting but have different goals. In a catalog they belong beside RSA, DSA and ECDSA, not merged into one “algorithm”.
