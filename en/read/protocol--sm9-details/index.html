SM9 Identity-Based Cryptography

Principles

Protocol: SM9 Identity-Based Cryptography (SM9 标识密码). ### Identity is the public key

SM9 is identity-based cryptography: a user’s public key is determined by an identity string (email, ID number, domain name), and the private key is derived by a key generation center (KGC) from the master secret and the identity. Separate signature and encryption master key pairs exist; the master public keys are public and the master secrets are held by the KGC. ### Bilinear pairing and signatures

SM9 uses a bilinear pairing e: G1×G2→GT on a 256-bit BN curve of prime order N. The signing key is dsA = [ks/(H1(IDA||hid,N)+ks)]P1. A signature (h,S) uses g=e(P1,Ppub-s), w=g^r, h=H2(M||w,N), l=(r−h) mod n and S=[l]dsA; verification recovers w from e(S, [H1(IDA||hid,N)]P2+Ppub-s). ### Encryption and key exchange

Encryption uses the recipient identity IDB and encryption master public key Ppub-e to compute a shared pairing value, KDF-derives a key, and outputs C1=[r]Q, C2 and C3. Key exchange computes a shared value from both identities and private keys, then derives a session key.

Why it matters

SM9’s value is removing certificate chains: the public key is the identity and the KGC derives private keys, which suits IoT, government, email and enterprise intranets where identities already exist. One identity-based standard family covers signatures, encryption, key exchange and KEM, reducing certificate issuance, distribution and revocation overhead.

The price is key escrow: the KGC knows or can generate every user’s private key, and master-key leakage compromises the whole system. It fits closed domains with centrally managed identities, not fully decentralized settings.

Applications

It is used for identity authentication, encrypted mail, electronic seals, IoT device identity and government/enterprise networks; outside TLCP, SM9 has separate signature, encryption and key-exchange flows. The standards are the GM/T 0044 series, raised to GB/T 38635 in 2020; identity-based encryption entered ISO/IEC 18033-5 in 2021.

Requirements: fix hid and curve parameters; protect or threshold-share the KGC master secret; distribute user private keys securely; check the h range and that S lies in G1; validate C1 lies in G1 during decryption.

Limits & attacks

SM9 security rests on bilinear Diffie–Hellman-type problems, SM3, the KGC master secret and user private keys. Single-point master-key leakage, a malicious KGC, key escrow, pairing side channels, identity-string ambiguity and private-key revocation are real risks.

Boundaries: SM9 is not a certificateless decentralized scheme; it concentrates trust in the KGC. Identity canonicalization (case, encoding, hid) must be consistent across systems, and one master key should not serve signature and encryption without domain separation.

History

Identity-based cryptography was proposed by Shamir in 1984. SM9 was organized by OSCCA: the GM/T 0044 series (five parts) appeared on 28 March 2016, and GB/T 38635.1/.2-2020 was published on 28 April 2020 and took effect in November 2020. SM9 signatures entered ISO/IEC 14888-3 in 2018; identity-based encryption entered ISO/IEC 18033-5 Amendment 1 in February 2021.

SM9’s significance is turning identity-based cryptography from a paper concept into an interoperable Chinese commercial standard with an international standards footprint.
