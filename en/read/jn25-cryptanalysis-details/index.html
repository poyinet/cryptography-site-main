JN-25 Cryptanalysis

Principles

Attack: JN-25 Cryptanalysis (JN-25 破译). ### Codebook and additive

JN-25 first looked up Japanese text in a five-digit codebook, producing code groups x_i; it then took five-digit groups k_i from an additive book and added them digitwise modulo 10:

c_i=(x_i+k_i) mod 10. This is not binary XOR but non-carrying decimal addition; the receiver subtracts digitwise with the same additive book. The book is organized by page and line, and an indicator group at the start of the message names the page and line used. ### Depth and codebook recovery

When several messages reuse the same additive page, subtract two ciphertexts digitwise:

c_i⊖c_i′=(x_i⊖x_i′) mod 10. The additive cancels, leaving differences of codebook groups. Enough depth, known code groups, guessed cribs and frequency analysis recover codebook meanings; new groups are then looked up and the plaintext grows.

Why it matters

JN-25 is a canonical codebook-plus-additive system. Its weakness is not the addition itself but additive-page reuse and a finite codebook; once depth is sufficient, statistical attack turns a one-time-looking mask into analyzable codebook differences.

VENONA also exploits repeated one-time-pad pages, but its failure is reuse of an OTP page; the JN-25 additive book was never an OTP, and reuse was normal, so depth accumulated more easily. The two are not the same key-reuse error.

Applications

  1. Read the indicator to identify the additive page and line. 2. Subtract the additive digitwise to obtain code groups. 3. Align messages using the same page to build depth. 4. Recover the codebook with known groups, cribs, frequency and traffic analysis. 5. Iterate: add newly read groups to the codebook and extend the next decryption pass.

Do not write JN-25’s digitwise mod-10 addition as XOR; do not ignore indicators and the garble check; do not call a partial codebook recovery a fully readable message.

Limits & attacks

  1. Additive-book change: a new page or book destroys existing depth and forces a new accumulation. 2. Codebook growth: new groups have no known meaning and must be guessed from context. 3. Indicator or copying errors: digitwise subtraction produces garbage and needs a garble check. 4. Not a modern cipher: the codebook-plus-additive structure is not equivalent to a modern stream or block cipher, and the historical break must not be extrapolated to OTP or AES.

History

JN-25 entered service in 1939; from 1940 the British Far East Combined Bureau and the U.S. OP-20-G worked on it. By the additive-book change in August 1941, the Americans had recovered about 2,000 code groups, roughly 4% of 27,500, mostly numerals and stereotypes; another book change on 4 December 1941 forced the work to restart.

Breaking JN-25 was incremental; no single battle result should be attributed to one decrypt. This entry covers the cryptographic mechanism, scale and progress, not tactical outcomes as algorithmic conclusions.
