Groth16 zk-SNARK

Principles

Algorithm: Groth16 zk-SNARK (Groth16 证明系统). ### From R1CS to three group elements

Groth16 is a preprocessing zk-SNARK for arithmetic circuits. The circuit is encoded as R1CS (one multiplication constraint per row), then converted through a Quadratic Arithmetic Program (QAP) into polynomial equations. Using a common reference string (CRS), the prover commits to the witness polynomials and outputs three bilinear-group elements A, B, C. The verifier checks one pairing-product equation plus a linear combination of public inputs. The proof reveals nothing about the witness, and verification is essentially independent of circuit size — the efficiency breakthrough of 2016.

Why it matters

Groth16 compresses “this computation is correct” into a few hundred bytes and a handful of pairings. Compared with earlier Pinocchio-style systems, it reduced the proof to three group elements and verification to a single equation, making it attractive for recursive proofs and on-chain verification.

The price is a circuit-specific CRS. Change the circuit and the parameters must be regenerated, so Groth16 suits fixed circuits rather than frequently changing universal virtual machines.

Applications

Groth16 became one of the most common backends for privacy transactions and early zk-Rollups; Zcash Sapling uses a Groth16-based proving system. It is also used as an inner proof in recursive constructions because proofs are small and verification is cheap.

In practice it appears with Circom, snarkjs, bellman and similar toolchains. Proving remains expensive; verification is light.

Limits & attacks

The main risk is the circuit-specific trusted setup. If the “toxic waste” in the CRS is not destroyed, its holder can forge proofs; every circuit change needs a new ceremony.

It also relies on R1CS encoding, pairing-friendly curves and random-oracle/generic-group assumptions; it is not post-quantum. Prover work grows with circuit size and needs parallelism for very large circuits.

History

Jens Groth published “On the Size of Pairing-based Non-interactive Arguments” at EUROCRYPT 2016, giving a three-element proof, a single verification equation, and a lower bound ruling out one-element SNARGs in the generic asymmetric bilinear-group model.

The paper does not prove that three elements are the absolute minimum; it explicitly leaves open whether two-element proofs can be ruled out. Groth16 later became the benchmark for engineering zk-SNARKs.
