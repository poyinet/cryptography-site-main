IEEE 802.1AR-2018 Secure Device Identity

Article review and catalog-metadata acceptance are recorded separately. The library remains under construction.

PrinciplesWhy it mattersApplicationsLimits & attacksHistoryWorked example & verificationReferences

Principles

Standard: Secure Device Identity (安全设备标识). IEEE 802.1AR-2018 defines a Secure Device Identifier (DevID) for network devices: an X.509 certificate bound to a device private key. IDevID is manufacturer-provided at production, globally unique and protected from modification; LDevID is issued by a local administrator or domain CA when the device joins a domain. The goal is to let a device prove “which device I am” without a human login, using EAP-TLS, BRSKI/EST and similar protocols.

Why it matters

Device identity is not a certificate filename but proof of private-key possession. The IDevID private key normally sits in a secure element and cannot be exported; LDevID must bind to the same device IDevID, otherwise copying an LDevID to another device would impersonate it.

The binding is maintained by the IDevID certificate fingerprint/public key and the enrollment proof; the verifier also checks the LDevID path to the domain trust anchor and revocation status.

Applications

Typical flow: the manufacturer burns in IDevID and private key; at domain join the device presents the IDevID certificate and signs a nonce/domain ID with the IDevID key; the domain CA verifies and issues LDevID; later EAP-TLS uses LDevID for device authentication.

Certificate fields follow RFC 5280; serial, vendor and model can sit in subject or SAN otherName; the 2018 revision adds the ECDSA P-384/SHA-384 suite for Suite B alignment.

Limits & attacks

Common errors include treating a certificate as an authorization token, validating LDevID without the IDevID binding, ignoring revocation and time, copying the IDevID private key like a normal file, or treating 802.1AR as EAP itself.

802.1AR defines identity and binding only; business roles, access policy and the EAP method are supplied by 802.1X, RADIUS/AAA, BRSKI and local policy.

History

IEEE 802.1AR-2018 was approved by the IEEE SA Board on 2018-06-14 and published on 2018-08-02, superseding 802.1AR-2009. The 2018 revision adds the ECDSA P-384/SHA-384 signature suite and refines IDevID/LDevID certificate fields and supplier information.

The IEEE GET program provides a free standard PDF; the later 802.1AReg amendment is still in progress and must not be treated as current text.
