KISS Pseudorandom Number Generator

Article review and catalog-metadata acceptance are recorded separately. The library remains under construction.

PrinciplesWhy it mattersApplicationsLimits & attacksHistoryWorked example & verificationReferences

Principles

Algorithm: KISS Pseudorandom Number Generator (KISS 伪随机数生成器). KISS combines four 32-bit sub-generators into one 32-bit output over a 128-bit state. MWC updates two 16-bit halves: (z'=36969,(z\bmod 2^{16})+\lfloor z/2^{16}\rfloor), (w'=18000,(w\bmod 2^{16})+\lfloor w/2^{16}\rfloor), then shifts (z') left by 16 and adds (w'). SHR3 applies three shift-xor steps: left 17, right 13, left 5. CONG is (x'=69069x+1234567\bmod 2^{32}). The output is ((MWC\oplus x')+SHR3\bmod 2^{32}). The sub-generator periods constrain one another; a single LCG period (2^{32}) is not the whole period.

Why it matters

KISS means “Keep It Simple, Stupid” and targets a very fast, very short PRNG for simulation and statistical tests, not a block cipher. It has no key, authentication or side-channel goal; using KISS as a stream cipher or CSPRNG mistakes “statistically random” for “unpredictable”. This card also corrects the old catalog label “lightweight block cipher”: KISS updates a PRNG state, and its output is not (E_K(x)).

Applications

An implementation fixes four 32-bit initial states, never all zero, then updates MWC, SHR3 and CONG in the same order and combines the outputs. To verify, reproduce the first few 32-bit outputs from a known seed, then check the (x') wrap at (2^{32}), the 16-bit split in MWC and the SHR3 shift directions. It suits Monte Carlo, game drafts and test data; it does not suit keys, nonces, session tokens or anything requiring unpredictability.

Limits & attacks

Rose gives two key boundaries: the 32-bit KISS maximum period is about (2^{119.5}), not the earlier (2^{123}); about 70 output words recover the state with complexity near (2^{29.1}). A long period is therefore not cryptographic security. The 64-bit/256-bit variants (KISS64/KISS11) have a larger state and period near (2^{247}), but remain statistical PRNGs; their parameters must not be mixed into the 32-bit formulas.

History

Marsaglia and Zaman proposed the KISS-style combination in 1993; the 1998 C version combined LCG, SHR3 and two MWC components into a four-word state; Rose’s 2018 analysis established the period bound and state-recovery attack. The 2009 64-bit KISS expanded the state to 256 bits and a period near (2^{247}). R128 also changes the catalog title from “lightweight block cipher” to “pseudorandom number generator”, because the old classification contradicts the algorithm.
