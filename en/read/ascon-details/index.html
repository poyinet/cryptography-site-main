ASCON Lightweight AEAD and Hash

Article review and catalog-metadata acceptance are recorded separately. The library remains under construction.

Principles

Algorithm: ASCON (ASCON 轻量级密码族). ### 320-bit state and SPN permutation

All Ascon modes share a 320-bit state split into five 64-bit words. Each round has three steps: XOR a round constant into S2, apply 64 parallel 5-bit S-boxes, and diffuse within each word by XORing rotated copies. AEAD initialization/finalization use 12 rounds (p[12]); data blocks use 8 rounds (p[8]). ### Duplex AEAD

Ascon-AEAD128 uses 128-bit keys, nonces and tags, with a 128-bit rate and 192-bit capacity. Initialization injects K||N; AD and plaintext are injected in 128-bit blocks while ciphertext is extracted, with p[8] after each block; finalization injects K again and outputs a 128-bit tag. ### Hash and XOF

Ascon-Hash256 absorbs 64-bit blocks and outputs 256 bits; Ascon-XOF128 produces arbitrary-length output. They share the same permutation as the AEAD mode.

Why it matters

ASCON is NIST’s lightweight cryptography standard: on IoT, RFID, sensors and 8/16/32-bit MCUs, AES-GCM table or instruction overhead can be too high, while Ascon uses only AND/XOR/rotations and a 5-bit S-box, giving a small hardware footprint and bitsliced software.

One permutation covers AEAD, hashing and XOF, reducing the number of cryptographic primitives in a chip or firmware image.

Applications

It is used for Ascon-AEAD128, Ascon-Hash256, Ascon-XOF128 and Ascon-CXOF128; NIST SP 800-232 was published in August 2025. It suits short messages, low power and area-constrained devices.

Requirements: unique nonces; truncation only down to 96/128 bits; p[12] for initialization/finalization and p[8] for data, never mixed; masked implementations for side channels; do not treat it as a high-throughput replacement for AES-GCM.

Limits & attacks

Ascon-AEAD128 is a nonce-based AEAD: encrypting different messages under the same key and nonce breaks confidentiality and authenticity; nonce reuse is the most direct attack surface.

Boundaries: 128-bit security is not 256-bit; tag truncation lowers forgery cost; power/EM side channels on low-power devices need masking; Ascon software is slower than AES-NI on 64-bit servers, so choose by platform.

History

ASCON was designed by Dobraunig, Eichlseder, Mendel and Schläffer, submitted to CAESAR in 2014 and selected for the CAESAR lightweight AEAD portfolio in 2019. NIST announced the Ascon family as its lightweight cryptography standard in February 2023, released the initial SP 800-232 draft in November 2024 and the final standard in August 2025.

Its design targets low area, bitsliced friendliness and online authentication; the standard mainly uses the 12/8-round permutations.
