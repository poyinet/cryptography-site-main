Regev's Reduction

The text and catalog metadata passed current review.

PrinciplesWhy it mattersApplicationsLimits & attacksHistoryWorked example & verificationReferences

Principles

Theorem: Regev's Reduction (Regev 归约). Regev proves a quantum reduction from worst-case GapSVP—deciding whether a lattice has a vector shorter than a bound—to average-case LWE samples; conversely, any LWE solver solves the corresponding GapSVP. The quantum step prepares lattice-related quantum states, while the protected encryption scheme remains classical.

Why it matters

It gives LWE a worst-case anchor: breaking LWE means solving worst-case lattices, not merely one random instance. Post-quantum standardisation needs assumptions that can be formalised; otherwise parameters rest on experience alone. It is a core argument for why lattice cryptography is considered quantum-resistant.

Restate the chain as input → state → transform → output → criterion. Every step should identify who acts, what changes and what evidence remains. A step without evidence is marked unverified, not reproduced.

Applications

As the security-proof template and starting point for parameter evaluation in LWE/Ring-LWE schemes. Production parameters also use lattice estimators, but the reduction gives an asymptotic floor, not a direct bit count.

Limits & attacks

A reduction is not an attack and does not prove GapSVP absolutely hard. It is quantum, but only for the proof; it does not imply a quantum attack exists. Approximation factors can be large and the reduction non-tight; noise distribution, modulus and dimension affect the conclusion. It does not cover implementation side channels.

History

Regev gave the reduction at STOC 2005 and introduced LWE in the same paper. Peikert later improved classical reductions, and Lyubashevsky et al. extended the approach to rings. It turned lattice security from intuition into a citable theorem.
