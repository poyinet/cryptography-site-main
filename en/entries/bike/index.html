Principles

Algorithm: BIKE (BIKE 备选 KEM). ### Key, encapsulation and decapsulation

BIKE works in the polynomial ring modulo x^r−1 with h=h1·h0^{-1}, and encapsulation forms c=(e0+e1h,m⊕L(e0,e1)). ### QC-MDPC structure

The private key is a pair of sparse polynomials (h0,h1), each of weight w/2; the code length is n=2r, the error vector (e0,e1) has total weight t, and decapsulation uses decoder(c0h0,h0,h1) to recover e′ and check e′=H(m′). ### Parameters and sizes

NIST Round-4 parameters: Level 1 r=12323, w=142, t=134; Level 3 r=24659, w=206, t=199; Level 5 r=40973, w=274, t=264. LibOQS 5.1 reports BIKE-L1 public key 1541 B, secret key 5223 B, ciphertext 1573 B and shared secret 32 B.

Why it matters

BIKE is a code-based KEM whose security rests on quasi-cyclic syndrome decoding and code distinguishing; its public key is far smaller than McEliece, and encapsulation/decapsulation are fast, suiting ephemeral TLS keys.

It needs one symmetric primitive (Keccak/SHAKE) and polynomial arithmetic, giving a small hardware footprint. As a NIST alternate it offers assumption diversity from lattice cryptography.

Applications

BIKE entered NIST PQC Round 4 as an alternate candidate, and liboqs provides a C implementation; typical uses are ephemeral TLS/QUIC KEMs, hybrid key exchange and research comparison.

Because NIST did not standardize it, production should use a hybrid KEM and wait for standards and side-channel audits; it is not an approved algorithm.

Limits & attacks

The decoding failure rate must be provably low (targets 2^-128/192/256); otherwise reaction attacks can recover the private key. The decoder must be constant-time against timing and faults.

Key reuse, multi-target attacks, weak private polynomials and a wrong FO transform break IND-CCA. BIKE’s proof relies on an ideal decoder and random-oracle assumptions, not unconditional security.

History

BIKE grew from QC-MDPC codes and the Niederreiter framework and was submitted to NIST PQC in 2017; after Rounds 2/3/4 and several parameter and decoder revisions, the 2022/2023 v5.x specification fixed a single variant and the BGF decoder.

It advanced the engineering of code-based KEMs but was not selected for NIST standardization; its parameter and decoder research continues to influence schemes such as HQC.
