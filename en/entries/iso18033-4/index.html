Encryption algorithms — Part 4: Stream ciphers

The text and catalog metadata passed current review.

PrinciplesWhy it mattersApplicationsLimits & attacksHistoryWorked example & verificationReferences

Principles

Standard: Encryption algorithms — Part 4: Stream ciphers (流密码算法标准). The generic stream-cipher interface is (KS=G(K,IV,state)), with state advanced by (state_{t+1}=Next(state_t)); binary-additive output is (C_t=P_t\oplus KS_t) and decryption is (P_t=C_t\oplus KS_t). ISO/IEC 18033-4:2011 separates output functions from generators: two output functions, binary-additive and MULTI-S01; five generators, MUGI, SNOW 2.0, Rabbit, Decimv2 and KCipher-2. Amendment 1:2020 adds ZUC, making six generators; ZUC uses a 128-bit key (K) and a 128-bit (IV). A synchronous stream cipher derives the keystream only from (K,IV); a self-synchronizing one also uses past ciphertext.

Why it matters

In the 一次性密码本与流密码 reading path, Encryption algorithms — Part 4: Stream ciphers connects to government contexts; the main risks are protocol, implementation.

Applications

Choose a generator and output function, then check (K), (IV) lengths and uniqueness. Binary-additive output is a bitwise XOR and suits streaming; MULTI-S01 needs the standard multi-block combiner and must not be treated as plain XOR. If the keystream repeats, XOR leaks plaintext directly, so every session needs a fresh IV and the same key must not reuse an IV. For integrity, add a MAC or use an AEAD outside the stream cipher; do not rely on the stream cipher alone.

Limits & attacks

There are four boundaries. First, binary-additive output is malleable: flipping a ciphertext bit flips the corresponding plaintext bit, and the standard supplies no authentication. Second, IV reuse repeats the keystream and is catastrophic; the standard defines the IV interface but does not manage IVs for the application. Third, the standard lists algorithms and interfaces, not security proofs; claims for MUGI, SNOW 2.0, Rabbit, Decimv2, KCipher-2 and ZUC must go back to their design analyses and later cryptanalysis. Fourth, ZUC is the 2020 amendment, so the 2011 “five” must not be silently rewritten as “six” without a version note.

History

ISO/IEC 18033-4:2005 first listed MUGI and SNOW 2.0 and gave OFB, CTR and CFB keystream constructions from block ciphers; the 2011 second edition expanded to five dedicated generators and kept the binary-additive and MULTI-S01 output functions. In August 2020, ZUC was published as Amendment 1, becoming the sixth generator. The version difference is preserved from the ISO pages rather than back-dated into the 2011 body.
