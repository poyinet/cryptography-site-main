Encryption algorithms — Part 3: Block ciphers

The text and catalog metadata passed current review.

PrinciplesWhy it mattersApplicationsLimits & attacksHistoryWorked example & verificationReferences

Principles

Standard: Encryption algorithms — Part 3: Block ciphers (分组密码算法标准). ISO/IEC 18033-3:2010 specifies block-cipher primitives, not modes. The generic interface is (C=E_K(P)), (P=D_K(C)) with (|P|=|C|=b). Seven algorithms form two families: 64-bit blocks TDEA, MISTY1, CAST-128 and HIGHT; 128-bit blocks AES, Camellia and SEED. TDEA is (C=E_{K_3}(D_{K_2}(E_{K_1}(P)))), the EDE composition of three DES calls. One AES round applies SubBytes, ShiftRows and MixColumns, then AddRoundKey; the number of rounds is 10 for a 128-bit key, 12 for 192 and 14 for 256.

Why it matters

In the d08-block-ciphers reading path, Encryption algorithms — Part 3: Block ciphers connects to government contexts; the main risks are protocol, implementation.

Applications

In engineering, choose the block size from data lifetime first: new systems should prefer 128-bit blocks and keep 64-bit blocks for legacy interoperability. Then choose the key size from the security target. TDEA with a 128-bit key is 2-key (about 112-bit strength); 192-bit is 3-key (about 168-bit), so key bits are not the same as security strength. After choosing the primitive, enter the ISO/IEC 10116 mode layer: CBC needs an unpredictable IV, CTR/GCM needs a unique nonce, and GCM also needs additional authenticated data. This standard supplies none of those by itself.

Limits & attacks

There are four boundaries. First, the standard defines primitives only; it does not define modes, padding, IV generation or authentication, so treating it as a complete encryption scheme loses integrity. Second, a 64-bit block reaches a birthday collision bound near (2^{32}) blocks; TDEA, MISTY1, CAST-128 and HIGHT should not carry new long-lived or high-volume data. Third, 2-key TDEA is about 112-bit strength and 3-key about 168-bit; do not advertise 128/192-bit strength. Fourth, the standard does not assess implementation side channels, key storage or protocol composition; those need separate audits.

History

The 2005 edition specified TDEA, MISTY1, CAST-128, AES, Camellia and SEED; the 2010 second edition added HIGHT, giving four 64-bit and three 128-bit algorithms. AES comes from FIPS 197; Camellia and SEED are widely used 128-bit block ciphers from Asian standardization; MISTY1, CAST-128 and HIGHT remain in the 64-bit family. The account follows the ISO abstract and the preview algorithm/key-length table, and does not turn “listed in a standard” into “still recommended”.
