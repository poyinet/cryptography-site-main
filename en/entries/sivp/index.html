Shortest Independent Vector Problem

The text and catalog metadata passed current review.

Principles

Algorithm: Shortest Independent Vector Problem (最短独立向量问题). SIVP asks, given an n-dimensional lattice, for n linearly independent lattice vectors whose longest vector is as short as possible; the approximation version only requires a factor γ from optimal. It is closely related to SVP (find one shortest nonzero vector): an SIVP solution gives a basis, and an SVP solution helps build short vectors. Basis-reduction algorithms such as LLL and BKZ solve approximations.

Why it matters

Lattice cryptography usually reduces to approximate SVP, SIVP or GapSVP. Understanding SIVP explains why basis quality affects decryption and signing: a bad basis produces large coefficients, noise growth and decryption failure. It is also the entry point for estimating lattice-attack cost.

Applications

As a hardness problem in security proofs, a target for basis reduction and a reference for parameter evaluation. It produces no ciphertext; implementations estimate attack complexity with BKZ and related algorithms.

Limits & attacks

Approximate SIVP is solvable in low dimensions with LLL/BKZ; exact high-dimensional instances are believed hard. Larger approximation factors make the problem easier, so parameters must keep known attacks below the target security level. It is not “unsolvable”, only without a known polynomial algorithm.

History

SIVP was studied as a classical lattice problem in the 1990s; Micciancio and Goldwasser organised its complexity and cryptographic connections in a 2002 monograph. Reductions by Ajtai–Dwork, Regev and others linked it to LWE. It is standard vocabulary in lattice cryptography.
