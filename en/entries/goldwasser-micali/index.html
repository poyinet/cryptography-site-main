Goldwasser-Micali Probabilistic Encryption

The text and catalog metadata passed current review.

Principles

Theorem: Goldwasser-Micali Probabilistic Encryption (Goldwasser-Micali 概率加密). ### One bit at a time

Goldwasser–Micali (GM) encrypts a single bit per operation. The public key is (N,x), where N=pq and x is a quadratic non-residue modulo N; the private key is p,q. To encrypt bit m_i, pick a random y and compute c_i=y²·x^{m_i} mod N: bit 0 yields a quadratic residue, bit 1 a non-residue. ### Decryption and homomorphism

The holder of p,q decides whether c_i is a quadratic residue modulo N and recovers the bit. Multiplying two ciphertexts XORs the bits: E(b1)·E(b2)=E(b1⊕b2). GM is XOR-homomorphic, not additively homomorphic. ### Security definition

GM’s importance is not efficiency but the first provable definition of probabilistic public-key encryption and semantic security: under the quadratic residuosity assumption, the ciphertext leaks no polynomially computable information about the plaintext.

Why it matters

GM answered the question “how secure should public-key encryption be?” Before it, “the attacker cannot recover the plaintext” was often the standard. GM introduced semantic security: given a ciphertext, the adversary cannot guess any function of the plaintext better than guessing without the ciphertext.

Its second contribution is probabilistic encryption: every encryption of the same bit differs, so an attacker cannot re-encrypt candidate plaintexts and compare. The modern IND-CPA definition grew out of this line.

Applications

Because of its expansion, GM is rarely used for bulk data. Its value is in theoretical constructions and protocol subroutines: zero-knowledge proofs, secure multiparty computation, private information retrieval and small XOR-homomorphic circuits.

Read this entry as a milestone of semantic security, not as a recommended production cipher.

Limits & attacks

GM encrypts one bit into roughly |N| bits; a message expands by hundreds of times and encryption is slow. It supports XOR homomorphism only, not ordinary addition or multiplication.

The original scheme is not IND-CCA2 secure. Implementations must also ensure x is a quadratic non-residue, N is a product of two large primes, and the random y is coprime to N. Real systems do not use GM in place of AES or RSA-OAEP.

History

Goldwasser and Micali presented “Probabilistic Encryption & How to Play Mental Poker, Keeping Secret All Partial Information” at STOC 1982 and published the journal version “Probabilistic Encryption” in Journal of Computer and System Sciences 28(2):270–299 in 1984.

The paper established the language of semantic security, probabilistic encryption and polynomial-time indistinguishability. Later IND-CPA and IND-CCA definitions extended this tradition.
